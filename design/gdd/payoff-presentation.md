# Payoff Presentation

> **Status**: Designed (CD-GDD-ALIGN CONCERNS applied 2026-11-14; independent `/design-review` not yet run)
> **Author**: user + Claude design session, with specialist consultation
> **Last Updated**: 2026-11-14 (Skip revision, concept fourth review)
> **Last Verified**: 2026-11-13
> **Implements Pillar**: The Payoff Is the Product (small triggers stay quiet, big chains are unmistakable, and the clearer cause wins); Arrangement Is the Thinking (the player can trace what they arranged to what happened)

## Summary

Payoff Presentation plays a finished fight back to the player. The fight is resolved before playback starts and recorded in a sealed trace, so this system is a reader: a **playback director** moves one cursor through the trace at 1×, 2×, 4× or Skip, an **effect presenter** turns each root action's size into a four-step ladder (Tick, Chain, Surge, Detonation) of light, numerals and motion that never covers the grid, the HP or the intent, and a small **placeholder sound layer** of four cues rises with the same ladder. Everything shown is a function of committed records and their historical views; speed, pause and Skip change presentation only, never the trace, the outcome or the reward hand-off. A Detonation (a large Seal detonation, or a rare very large chain) is the peak: it plays at full timing on every speed, Skip never passes over it (Skip fast-forwards to the next Detonation, plays it in full, then continues), and it holds the contributing item plates lit afterwards so the cause is never lost in the cheer. In the boss fight the blow that takes a large share of the boss's health gets a beat of its own (Rule 21).

> **Quick reference** — Layer: `Presentation` · Priority: `MVP` · Key deps: `Event Log (4), Combat State and Step Model (36), Effect Resolution Model (3), Combat Resolution (12), Grid Backpack (8), Backpack UI (25), Settings (37)`

## Overview

Combat in Grimpack is automatic and resolves synchronously: System 12 runs the whole fight, System 4 seals one append-only trace per encounter, and System 36 supplies an immutable historical view after every record. Playback is therefore a reading problem, not a simulation problem. System 29 owns three layers that read the same cursor. The **playback director** (engine-free) owns the cursor, the pacing, the speed, the pause and the Skip, and tells System 5 once that playback is finished. The **effect presenter** classifies each root action by size and draws the matching step of the escalation ladder: avatars, threads, rings, chips, numerals, the Candle wash and the bloom, all beneath the protected layer of the Combat HUD (System 27) and the workbench (System 25). The **placeholder sound layer** plays four cues from an asset pack, escalating with the same ladder, until System 32 replaces it in the Vertical Slice. System 29 does not resolve combat, choose targets, draw RNG, write to Systems 3, 4 or 36, own the HUD (System 27), the grid view (System 25), the popup (System 26), the breakdown (System 30), the input mapping (System 6), the speed setting (System 37) or the flow (System 5). Without it the MVP could not be judged on its central question, whether the payoff lands; with it the player watches their own arrangement fire and can see why (Pillar 2).

Their findings are applied below; where they disagreed the user decided (Core Rules 4, 7, 9, 11, 12).*

## Player Fantasy

The player has done their thinking before the fight. Now they watch it pay. A single item firing is a murmur: a pulse on its plate, a small avatar, a soft tick. A chain is a rising run of lights, each link tracing back to the plate that caused it. And when a Seal detonation lands, the room holds still for a breath, the last three avatars gather into a fan, and the whole column blooms warm. Then the bloom fades and the plates that did it stay lit, one after another, in the order they mattered. **When the machine fires, the player sees their own arrangement do it: small things stay quiet, big chains swell, and at the peak every piece that mattered lights up once more, so the cause is never lost in the cheer.**

*Pillar anchors.* Pillar 2, The Payoff Is the Product: escalation in sight, sound and feel with combo size; the design test is that the clearer cause wins over a bigger effect, so the bloom is dimmed before a thread or an echo is. Pillar 4, Arrangement Is the Thinking: the effects relay from the source plate in the grid, so the player can walk the cause backwards into their own layout. Pillar 5, Power Has a Price: the cost is never hidden. The enemy's moves and the damage the party takes are shown with the same readable numerals and glyphs; the escalation ladder measures only what the party's arrangement does to the enemy side.

*Guardrails:* escalation by adding, never by covering (the grid, HP, intent and turn marker stay above every effect); the ladder level depends only on the committed records of the action being shown; no information is carried by sound alone; speed, pause and Skip never change what happened; an effect that would break the flash limits degrades to its steady form instead of firing.

*Risks the design guards against:* spectacle that hides the cause; small triggers as loud as big ones; a 200-action fight that is tedious at 1×; Skip that deletes the payoff; a Detonation that feels the same every time; a flash rate that is unsafe at 4×; a presentation that quietly drifts from the trace.

## Detailed Design

### Core Rules

1. **A reader, not a simulator.** The fight is resolved and the trace is sealed before playback begins (System 5 Rule 6). System 29 reads committed records (System 4) and the immutable post-event view at a sequence number (System 36). It has no write path to Systems 3, 4, 12 or 36 (the presenter assembly references read-only interfaces only), draws no gameplay RNG, and never decides an outcome. Every displayed HP, shield, status and intent value comes from the view at the cursor, never from accumulated deltas, so Skip and full playback end on the same view.
2. **The cursor.** The cursor is an integer sequence number in `[0, sealedMax]` (0 means before the first record): the sequence of the last record whose beat has been fully presented. Only the director moves it, only forward, and only to a beat boundary, to a Skip stop (`firstSeq - 1` of a Detonation root action, which is a boundary) or to the end. Other systems (the HUD, the workbench, the effect presenter, the audio) read `IPlaybackView` (the cursor, the baseline, `ViewAt(seq)`, the current beat and its progress, the acting hero, the speed, `IsSkipping` (true only while the cursor fast-forwards in a Skip leg), `SkipArmed` (true from an accepted Skip until it is cancelled or the terminal view is reached), a `GenerationId` bumped at every cursor jump and every Skip handover) and never advance it. Hover, Details or inspection never move the cursor (Combat HUD criterion 18).
3. **Beats.** A beat is the unit of pacing: `Beat { firstSeq, lastSeq, level, offsetMs, durationMs, kind, anchors }`. One effect, identified by `(rootActionId, effectOrdinal)`, is one beat; the records of one effect (a consumption and its damage steps, a shield step and an HP step) are one atomic batch inside it. Unstamped records (`ActionStarted`, `ActionSkipped`, `StatusExpired`, `UnitDowned`, `CombatEnded`) have their own beats, and a fact record (`ChainLimit`, `ListenerSuppressed`, `EffectSkipped`, `DetonationEmpty`) attaches a short beat to its effect. `BuildSchedule(records[a..b], speed, pacing)` is a pure function of its inputs: no clock, no RNG, no `Dictionary` iteration order; it is built for the whole encounter at `Begin` (a 200-action fight is small), and a speed change rebuilds only the future beats.
4. **Combo size and the ladder (user decision).** The size of a **root action** (all records sharing a `rootActionId`) is classified from its committed records only: `N` is the count of committed mutating effects, excluding `EffectSkipped`, `ListenerSuppressed` and `DetonationEmpty`; `depthMax` is the largest `depth`; `D` is the actual shield lost plus HP lost on the enemy side by the root action, and `Hmax` is the sum of the maximum HP of the enemies alive when the action began (both from the views). The level is the highest that applies (default thresholds, data, provisional): **Tick** `N = 1`; **Chain** `N` in 2 to 4 or `depthMax ≥ 1`; **Surge** `N ≥ 5` or `depthMax ≥ 3`; **Detonation** either (a) a `DetonationDamage` record exists in the root action **and** (`N ≥ 6`, or `D × 100 ≥ 35 × Hmax`, or it is lethal to the last living enemy), or (b) the **size route**, for builds that do not use Seal (user decision): `N ≥ 12` or `depthMax ≥ 5` (provisional, about the 99th percentile; knobs). A Seal detonation that does not meet route (a) keeps its level from size (it is at least a Chain) and still plays the Detonate archetype of its effect (Rule 6); a chain with no detonation reaches Detonation only by route (b). The effect ordinal is a diagnostic only (gaps and skipped effects inflate it). The thresholds are tuned from the headless harness over simulated fights (Chain near the 60th percentile of `N`, Surge near the 90th, the size route near the 99th; aim for a median fight with no Detonation and about one per run, to avoid bloom fatigue), and live in data.
5. **Classification looks at one root action, never further (user decision).** Before presenting a root action the presenter classifies that whole action from its committed records, so the stillness beat and the build before a Detonation can be authored. It never reads `CombatEnded`, a later root action, a reward or the outcome, so nothing about the end of the fight is revealed. A technical-stop prefix classifies only the records it has.
6. **The ladder in light and motion (MVP).** Escalation is by adding, beneath the protected layer (grid, HP, intent, turn marker, order strip, action strip stay above everything). **Tick:** a 2-frame pulse on the source plate edge, then the plate echo held for the avatar's life; a quiet avatar (1 px ring, at most one 8 px chip, no particles). **Chain:** threads from each source plate to its avatar (1 to 2 px wide, 0 or 90 degrees, 4 px pitch, at most 3 live), avatar edge +1 step, 2 px ring, chips one size up (12 px numerals). **Surge:** a Candle wash over the right column that ramps in over at least 300 ms and fades over 400 ms (drawn with the stepped dither mask, never alpha blending), up to 60 pooled 1 to 3 px particles on impacts only, the contributing plates' echoes held for the whole surge, a 12 px numeral in a Bone frame. **Detonation:** a stillness beat of 150 to 200 ms (ambient scene motion pauses, nothing dims), a build in which the cadence accelerates to its 120 ms floor, the last up to three avatars forming a fan (the detonator and the largest consumed sources, taken from the `StatusConsumed` source list; for a size-route Detonation with no consumption, the last three avatars of the chain) that releases into a bloom of three stepped shells (a lit area of at most 25 percent for at most 1.5 s, one rise and one fall, the bright core inside a 160 by 160 px region), then an afterglow of 1 to 2 s with at most 80 embers. The Detonation particle total is at most 150 in the MVP. **Cause proof:** during the afterglow every contributing source plate holds its echo, ordered by the amount it contributed to the consumption (for a size-route Detonation, by the damage each plate's effects dealt), so the player can read the cause after the cheer. A Seal detonation (Detonate archetype) plays: the avatar pulses; the target's Seal chip count falls in at most 4 steps over 240 ms, each step sending a 1 to 2 hop mote from the chip to the body centre so the stacks visibly leave; a 48 px 4-frame burst fires at the body centre; one aggregated numeral shows the damage; with several targets the bursts are staggered 60 to 80 ms apart in line order so only one bright core is rising at a time.
7. **Numerals and chips.** The numeral size follows the level: Tick 8 px, Chain 12 px, Surge 12 px with a Bone frame, Detonation 16 px per hit and one aggregate 24 px numeral per target (the 16 and 24 px cuts are 2× integer scales of the 8 and 12 px faces and need sign-off in art bible 8.9). The size follows the level and, for the aggregate only, the damage fraction. Chips to the same target and stat within 300 ms aggregate; at most 2 chips per target at once; at most 8 aggregated chips at a Detonation. Numerals spawn at the body centre, rise inside the body band and sit below the protected layer; they may overlap a neighbouring body but never a tab or an HP row.
8. **Pacing and speed.** Speeds are 1×, 2×, 4× and Skip; speed is a multiplier on holds and cadence, never a different animation. The cadence of a trigger is `max(120, min(base, 3500 / Q))` ms with `base` 260 (Pulse, Swing, Status), 320 (Throw, Detonate) or 400 (Ring) and `Q` the triggers in the hero's queue; the cadence budget of one hero turn is 3500 ms (about 4 s with the ready and tail phases), and beats beyond 29 (`3500 / 120`) would exceed it. **Merging beats past 29 is deferred ([VS])** until the harness shows real queues that long; until then a queue plays unmerged at the cadence floor. At 2× holds and cadence are halved, no key frame shorter than 80 ms; at 4× avatars appear in groups of up to 3, each group held 160 ms, and chips aggregate per target. **Every luminance rise (the avatar pop-in, a thread, the bloom rise, the workbench swap) keeps at least 100 ms (6 frames) at every speed.** A speed change applies from the next beat boundary, keeping the progress of the current beat as a fraction. The default speed is 1× on the first run and the last chosen speed is remembered (System 37); Skip is never remembered.
9. **Level-aware pacing for long fights (user decision).** A skipped, stunned or no-effect action plays in at most 300 ms; an action whose root action is Tick-only plays at twice the chosen speed, capped at an effective 4× (so 1× plays Tick-only at 2×, and 2× and 4× play it at 4×, where the group rule of Rule 8 applies), respecting the floors in Rule 8; a Chain or higher plays at the chosen speed; a **Detonation always plays its authored timing**, defined as the stillness beat, the bloom and the afterglow (the build before it scales with the speed). The target is a median fight of 90 s or less at 1× (to be verified against measured action counts; see Open Questions). Beats are never removed, only shortened or merged, and a merged group still shows its aggregate chip.
10. **The Detonation peak at every speed (user decision).** The bloom and its afterglow play at 1× timing at 1×, 2× and 4× (the build is compressed, the stillness beat and the bloom are not). Two flash-class events (the bloom, or any other rise of 10 percent or more of the scene's maximum relative luminance) are never closer than 500 ms, and at most 2 may fall in any rolling 1000 ms (Formula 6): a Detonation that would break the rule degrades to Surge. The Candle wash is a swell of at most about 0.08 and is **not** flash-class, so a Surge is never refused. Rises are eased over at least 6 frames; the Candle wash changes the scene's relative luminance by no more than about 0.08. The same holds inside a Skip Forced span (Rule 11): the bloom plays in full there, and consecutive Skip-driven blooms are at least about 1.65 s apart when a cut and an orient hold lie between them (afterglow 1000 + cut 202 + hold 300 + stillness 150 ms) and at least about 1.31 s apart when they are back to back (afterglow 1000 + one build group 160 + stillness 150 ms), both over the 500 ms gap, so Formula 6 cannot refuse one.
11. **Skip (user decision, revised 2026-11-14 by the concept's fourth review).** Skip (K, or the Skip button) is one press with no confirmation, available once System 6's input lock (Rule 7e, 250 ms after the Start that began playback) expires; a press before then is refused, not buffered, and only the key-down edge counts. It **arms** Skip, a latched mode that fast-forwards to the next stop and never past a Detonation; Skip is never remembered between encounters. **Stop set:** every root action whose level by Rule 4 is Detonation, computed from its committed records and before the flash budget (a Detonation the budget degrades to Surge still stops and plays its stillness beat and the Surge form); a Surge or lower never stops Skip. **Plan:** `BuildSkipPlan(schedule)` is a pure function (no clock, no RNG, no `Dictionary` order) returning the ordered segments `FF(from, to)` (a fast-forward leg) and `Forced(rootId, from, to)`; an FF ends at `firstSeq(root) - 1`, a Forced span is the whole root action (its ready phase, build, stillness beat, bloom, afterglow and cause proof) and ends at `lastSeq(root)`, empty FFs are dropped, and after the last stop one final FF reaches the terminal view (or the end of the valid prefix after a technical stop). If the current beat belongs to a Detonation root action when Skip is accepted, the rest of that root action is a Forced span; otherwise the rest of the beat is dropped and the cursor jumps. **A fast-forward leg** closes an open read-only intent Details, plays no per-event effect or cue, releases pooled effects and plate echoes, moves the cursor in one step (every displayed value comes from `ViewAt`, Rule 1), bumps `GenerationId`, plays one plate sweep (Formula 5), then holds for an orient hold (provisional 300 ms) with the text line "Skipped N actions" (`N` is the number of actions jumped; a text key for System 7); the final leg uses the settle sweep and is followed by the banner, which for a last-action Detonation waits for the end of its afterglow and then holds at least 1 s (Rule 18). **A Forced span** plays the workbench follow again (the acting hero, a 100 ms swap, so the cause-proof echoes show on the right grid), the build at the Rule 8 group rule of 4×, the stillness beat, the bloom and the afterglow at authored timing (Rules 9 and 10) and every cue in full; a further Skip press is ignored (a one-frame pressed acknowledgement with no luminance rise), and keys 1, 2, 3 or a speed segment cancel the arm from the next beat boundary but never cut a Forced span short. There is no stamp. **Accessibility option:** the Settings option "Skip goes straight to the end" (default off, owned by System 37) makes Skip one leg to the terminal view (the settle sweep, then the banner) that never stops at a Detonation, for players who cannot tolerate the forced bloom. Skip pressed during a technical-stop prefix still stops at the Detonations inside the prefix and then lands at its end in `Stopped`. Skip never changes the trace, the outcome or the reward hand-off, and the final view equals the view after full playback (byte for byte).
12. **Pause (user decision).** Opening the menu (Esc or the Settings button, System 6 Rule 10) stops the playback clock; closing it resumes after a 250 ms fade-in. Pause is a flag inside Playing (the speed is held at 0), not a separate simulation state. A window deactivation or capture loss (`InputReset`) pauses the clock and opens the menu; playback resumes only when the player closes it. The language can be changed only through the menu, so a language change is always made while paused: strings re-resolve in place and playback does not restart. An abandon dialog freezes the clock; on confirm the cursor is discarded and no outcome is delivered (System 5 owns the abandon).
13. **Playback states.** `Idle` (no encounter), `Playing` (with the paused flag, and with `SkipArmed` while a Forced span plays), `Skipping` (a fast-forward leg), `Terminal` (the cursor reached `CombatEnded`, or the final Skip leg landed), `Stopped` (a technical stop; the valid prefix has been presented). `Terminal` and `Stopped` are final for the encounter; only System 5 leaves them (the hand-off, or an abandon back to `Idle`). On reaching `Terminal` or `Stopped` the director raises `ViewChanged(t)` once so System 6's input lock starts then, and calls an idempotent `PlaybackFinished(encounterId)` through the `IPlaybackGate` port exactly once per encounter, from a natural end or from the final Skip leg, never both; a Skip stop or handover never sends it. A Begin on an unsealed trace is rejected. A technical stop plays the committed prefix at the chosen speed (Skip stops at its Detonations, then jumps to its end), shows no outcome and no Defeat, and hands a distinct "something went wrong" state to Systems 5 and 31.
14. **The frozen arrangement (answers the open question of Systems 5, 25 and 29).** The by-value frozen arrangement view (the placement baseline, the item instances, their origins and the adjacency links System 10 produced) travels with the encounter record System 12 hands to System 5 together with the trace and the baseline. During playback System 25 binds to `IPlaybackView.FrozenArrangement` and never reads the live grid, so a victory that already thawed the grid does not matter. System 5 releases the record after the playback gate and the breakdown (System 30 needs it for causal highlights); System 29 never releases it. Adjacency links are captured in the record and never recomputed at playback.
15. **One cursor, many readers.** Order per tick: the director advances, bumps `GenerationId`, then the binder fans out: the HUD reads `ViewAt(cursor)` (level-triggered), the workbench follows the acting hero at the start of its Ready phase (none during a Skip fast-forward leg; in a Forced span it follows the acting hero again), the effect presenter spawns the effects of beats crossed (edge-triggered, keyed by a beat id, idempotent) and the audio fires its cues once per beat id. A `lastFiredBeatId` high-water mark makes a hitch unable to double-fire. A frame delta is clamped to `MaxFrameDeltaMs` (100 by default), so the cursor cannot leap; after a clamp the director may cross several short beats in one frame, still emitting each for the HUD and the audio-of-record, while the presenter may drop the visual effects of beats already past.
16. **The placeholder sound layer (four cues).** Four asset-pack cues from one family with an intensity parameter: `sfx_payoff_tick` (a soft dry click, about 60 to 100 ms), `sfx_payoff_chain` (the tick plus a brighter tonal layer; stepping the pitch up one semitone per link, capped at +7, is **[VS]**), `sfx_payoff_surge` (a low swell with a shimmer, once when a root action crosses the Surge threshold) and `sfx_payoff_detonate` (one big full-spectrum hit with a long tail and the only sub-bass, once at the release into the bloom, preceded by a 150 ms dip of 3 dB that matches the stillness beat). Victory and defeat reuse the pack's stingers (one sample each, well below the Detonation); a downed unit reuses the tick at a lower pitch. Rules: a Tick-level cue is near-silent and never steals a voice; loudness is a pure function of level on loudness-normalised samples (targets Tick -30, Chain -26, Surge -22, Detonation -16 LUFS short-term, which Formula 7 expresses as gains; the Detonation is at least 4 LU louder than any other cue); at most 4 voices for the tick and chain family (the lowest level and oldest voice is stolen), 1 for Surge, 1 for the Detonation; the minimum retrigger interval is `max(60, cadence / speed)` ms, and at 4× one chain cue plays per group of 3 at the group's highest level; ducking other effects under the Detonation is **[VS]**; during a Skip fast-forward leg all per-event cues are suppressed, a Forced span plays every cue in full, and the outcome stinger follows the banner. Sound is never the only carrier: the level is visible, the damage is a numeral, and the outcome and the actor are text and form (Rule 18). Distinct per-type sounds, music, ambience and mastering are System 32's. The pack's licence, format and whether it supplies tonal, pitch-shiftable samples with usable tails must be settled before the design is locked (Open Questions).
17. **Reduced flashes and reduced motion (MVP, user decision).** Reduced flashes ships in the MVP, **on by default until the photosensitivity capture and analyser run (criterion 25) passes**; passing it and switching the default off is a written condition that must be met **before the go/no-go playtest**, so the payoff is judged on the real bloom. A short prompt is shown once at the first launch before the first fight (Systems 31 and 37 own the screen and the stored setting). When on, the bloom becomes a steady glow, particles and embers are removed, rings appear at their final radius in 3 steps, avatars fade in without the tier pulse, and at 2× and 4× pop-ins aggregate into one swell instead of discrete pulses; the information is unchanged. Reduced motion replaces thread travel, travelling motes, shudder, numeral pop and chip rise with fades of equal duration, and numerals fade to their final size. A "Reduced audio intensity" companion (no sub-bass, Detonation capped at about -22 LUFS) is **[VS]**, requested from System 37. Neither setting removes information or changes the schedule's timing, the cursor or the lock.
18. **No sound-only information; text carriers.** The outcome (Victory, Defeat, Draw) is a banner and a glyph, not a jingle; the acting hero or enemy is marked by the turn marker or outline; the level is the visual ladder plus the damage numeral; a muted playthrough must still identify the outcome and the actor. The final damage and the outcome hold for at least 1 s at any speed, and no text is dismissed faster than a reading-time budget (a budget for System 7, longer for long Vietnamese strings).
19. **Controls and focus.** The action strip shows four rectangular segments `1×`, `2×`, `4×` and `Skip` (Skip separated by a 1 px divider because it is an action, not a speed); the current speed is the selected segment (ring and fill, not colour alone); direct keys 1, 2, 3 select speed (and cancel an armed Skip) and K arms Skip (System 6 Rule 2; the art bible's single cycle chip is amended to a prompt chip per segment). The speed segment of the current speed has focus on entering playback; Space activates the focused segment and never skips. Only the speed segments, the read-only intent tabs and the menu are focusable during playback (System 6 Rule 9). Pause is the Settings button and Esc, not a fifth segment. Nothing in playback is timed: there is no timed input and the 250 ms lock is a minimum delay. The Skip segment is never disabled: idle it reads "Skip"; armed it reads "Skipping" with a static double-chevron glyph in a bracket (not a ring), so it is never mistaken for a selected speed, and a Forced span adds a small "held" mark; the label is the same whether or not a Detonation remains ahead, so it reveals nothing about the rest of the fight (Rule 5). Whether the Skip segment should be focusable (a keyboard and gamepad route) is an open question for System 6.
20. **Scope tiers.** The MVP builds the director, the four-level ladder (with the dithered Candle wash), the four cues, level-aware pacing, Skip to the next Detonation (Rule 11), the boss blow beat (Rule 21), pause, reduced flashes and reduced motion. In the MVP the bloom core is kept off enemy bodies (a minimal silhouette guarantee: a body is never covered by a lit core). Simplifications for a solo developer (CD-GDD-ALIGN, user decision): the whole schedule is built at `Begin`; the zero-allocation criterion is advisory; threads start at the grid column edge (one leg); beat merging past 29, pitch stepping, ducking and Reduced audio intensity wait. Deferred to the Vertical Slice and marked **[VS]**: the full backlit enemy silhouettes at the bloom, the two-leg thread join (spike S1), per-tier colour variety in the convergence, camera shake (not in the art bible; ask first), a 0.5× slow speed (rejected for the MVP by the user), distinct per-type cues and music (System 32), a Surge recap at Skip, the HP delta markers at a Skip cut, and a dedicated cue for the boss blow (System 32).
21. **The boss blow (MVP, owner decision of the concept's fourth review, 2026-11-14).** In the boss fight, the root action classified Detonation that dealt at least the share `run_defining_damage_share` of the boss's maximum HP (the registry owns the value; there is no literal here) and began while the boss still had at least that share left is the **boss blow** (Formula 9). It is decided from that root action's committed records and the views either side of it, never from the outcome (Rule 5), so a boss blow that does not end in a Victory is marked too; only System 30 may call it run-defining, and only after a Victory. One pure function `BossBlow` is shared with System 35 (harness criterion 16), and the boss unit is identified through System 14. Every root action that qualifies gets the beat, decided in order as it is shown (a boss that heals or regains shield, or a lower `X`, can make more than one qualify); choosing the largest one is left to System 30 after a Victory, because it needs the rest of the fight (Rule 5). The beat adds and never covers, and keeps exactly one bloom (no second flash-class event): a longer stillness beat (provisional 300 to 350 ms against 150 to 200), the share line "NN% of the boss" (a text key for System 7; `NN` is the share of Formula 9) in the 24 px numeral's Bone frame, the part of the boss's HP row that the blow will take outlined in Bone from the start of the stillness beat (so the stillness frame already differs from an ordinary Detonation's) and filled in stepped fashion over 240 ms at the release, the outline staying for the afterglow (a text carrier, not a luminance rise), the boss's hurt pose held for the afterglow, the cause-proof echoes held until the afterglow ends (up to 3 s), and, for a lethal blow, the banner waiting for the end of the afterglow. Under Reduced flashes the longer stillness and the line stay and the bloom is the steady glow; under Reduced motion the outline appears at its final state in two steps and the line fades in; none of it depends on luminance alone. Every duration here is provisional until the art director's mock-up measures it against art bible 4.10 and 4.12. A dedicated cue (System 32), a boss-centred shockwave, a backlit boss silhouette and camera shake are [VS] or deferred.

### States and Transitions

| Subject | State | Trigger | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Director | Idle | No encounter, or an abandon | Cursor discarded; `IPlaybackView` empty. |
| Director | Playing | `Begin(traceId)` on a sealed trace (from System 5) | Cursor at 0; schedule built whole at `Begin`; the default or remembered speed. |
| Director | Playing (paused) | Menu opened, or window deactivation | Clock stopped; on close, 250 ms fade-in, then resumes. |
| Director | Skipping (fast-forward leg) | Skip accepted, or a Forced span ended and a stop or the end is ahead | Details closed; no effects or cues; pools and echoes released; the cursor jumps to `firstSeq - 1` of the next Detonation root action (or to the end); `GenerationId` bumped; plate sweep (Formula 5), then the orient hold and "Skipped N actions". |

## Formulas

All time arithmetic is on integer milliseconds (floor division); no floats, no wall clock in the core. All values are provisional until the harness and the combat spike measure real fights.

**Formula 1: combo size and level.** For a root action `r`: `N(r)` = committed mutating effects (excluding `EffectSkipped`, `ListenerSuppressed`, `DetonationEmpty`); `depthMax(r)`; `D(r)` = Σ actual shield lost + HP lost on the enemy side; `Hmax(r)` = Σ max HP of the enemies alive when `r` began. `level(r) = Detonation` if (a `DetonationDamage` record exists and (`N ≥ 6` or `100·D ≥ 35·Hmax` or lethal to the last enemy)) or `N ≥ 12` or `depthMax ≥ 5`; else `Surge` if `N ≥ 5` or `depthMax ≥ 3`; else `Chain` if `N ≥ 2` or `depthMax ≥ 1`; else `Tick`. *Examples:* one item firing a single hit: `N = 1`, Tick. Three items chained, `depthMax = 2`: Chain. A chain of `N = 7` with no detonation: Surge; `N = 12` with no detonation: Detonation by the size route. A Seal detonation with `N = 3` that deals `D = 12` to enemies with `Hmax = 40`: `1200 < 1400`, so not Detonation by damage and not by size, it stays Chain (with the Detonate archetype). The same with `D = 14`: `1400 ≥ 1400`, Detonation.

| Variable | Type | Range | Source | Description |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `N` | int | 0 to 200 expected | System 4 stamps | Effects in the root action. |
| `depthMax` | int | `0..D` (System 3 depth limit) | System 4 | Deepest effect. |
| `D`, `Hmax` | int | 0 to 100,000 | System 36 views | Damage and enemy max HP. |

**Formula 2: cadence.** `Q` is the number of triggers in a hero's queue (not the `N` of Formula 1). `cadence(base, Q) = max(120, min(base, 3500 / Q))`; `scaled(base, Q, s) = max(80, cadence(base, Q) / s)` for `s` in {1, 2}; at `s = 4` the beats of a turn form `⌈Q / 3⌉` groups of 3 each held 160 ms. *Examples:* `cadence(260, 8) = 260`, scaled at 2× = 130; `cadence(320, 40) = max(120, min(320, 87)) = 120`; `cadence(400, 1000) = 120`; 8 triggers at 4× take `⌈8/3⌉ × 160 = 480` ms.

**Formula 3: queue cap and merging ([VS]).** The cadence budget of one hero turn at 1× is `Σ cadence ≤ 3500` ms (about 4 s with the ready and tail phases); the number of beats within it is at most `M = 3500 / 120 = 29`; triggers beyond `M` would be merged into aggregate beats once merging ships. *Example:* 40 effects at base 320: `40 × 120 = 4800 > 3500`, so with merging 29 beats are played and the other 11 merge into the last beat's aggregate chip; in the MVP all 40 play unmerged.

**Formula 4: level-aware duration.** `base_ms` is the archetype's effect duration (art bible 5.11, for example Swing 240). `duration(beat) = min(300, base_ms)` for a skipped, stunned or no-effect action; for a Tick-only root action the effective speed is `min(4, 2·s)`, so `base_ms / effective` (then Formula 2's floors, and the group rule at an effective 4×); `base_ms` scaled by the chosen speed for Chain and Surge; the authored timing for a Detonation. *Example:* a Tick-only Swing of 240 ms plays in 120 ms at 1× (effective 2×); at 2× and 4× it is effective 4× and Tick-only beats form groups of 3 held 160 ms.

**Formula 5: Skip cut.** For a fast-forward leg with `P` plates to sweep and at most `F` start frames: `k = ⌈P / F⌉` plates start per frame, `frames = ⌈P / k⌉`, and `T_cut(P, F) = 100 + 17 × frames` ms (6 frames, 100 ms, of easing per plate and 17 ms per frame); it is 0 when `P = 0` or the leg is empty. `F = 6` (`skip_cut_frames`) for a cut before a Detonation and `F = 20` (`skip_settle_frames`) for the final leg. *Examples:* `P = 36`, `F = 20`: `k = 2`, 18 frames, `100 + 17 × 18 = 406` ms (at most 440); `P = 36`, `F = 6`: `k = 6`, 6 frames, 202 ms.

| Variable | Type | Range | Source | Description |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `P` | int | 0 to 36 and over | The frozen arrangement | Plates to sweep. |
| `F` | int | 6 (cut) or 20 (final leg) | Data | Start frames. |
| `T_cut` | int ms | at most 202 (`F = 6`), at most 440 (`F = 20`) | Derived | Cut duration. |

**Formula 6: flash budget.** Flash-class events (a bloom or wash rise of at least 10 percent of the scene's maximum relative luminance) are stamped with the presentation tick; a new one is allowed only if the previous is at least 500 ms earlier and fewer than 2 fall in the last 1000 ms; a refused Detonation degrades to Surge (the Candle wash is a swell of at most about 0.08 and is not flash-class, so a Surge is never refused). *Example:* blooms at `t = 0` and `t = 400` ms: the second degrades; at `t = 600` it is allowed (gap 600, one in the window).

**Formula 7: audio gain.** On loudness-normalised samples the level targets of Rule 16 give `G = {200, 316, 501, 1000}` permille amplitude for Tick, Chain, Surge, Detonation (the offsets -14, -10, -6 and 0 dB from the Detonation at -16 LUFS); `gain_pm = G[level] × SG[s] / 100` with `SG = {1×: 100, 2×: 90, 4×: 75}`; the sample variant is `event_seq mod 3`. Pitch stepping (`min(700, 100 × (k − 1))` cents for the `k`-th link) is **[VS]**. *Example:* a Chain cue at 2×: gain `316 × 90 / 100 = 284` permille.

**Formula 8: Forced span and the Skip bound.** A Forced span lasts `T_F = T_ready + T_build + T_still + T_bloom + T_glow`; `T_ready` is the acting hero's ready phase, the same as in full playback, and is not bounded here. At the 4× group rule `T_build = ⌈Q / 3⌉ × 160` ms for a queue of `Q` triggers, and because beats merge past 29 only in the Vertical Slice, `Q` is not capped in the MVP. Without the ready phase, for `Q ≤ 29`: build at most 1600, stillness at most 200, bloom at most 1500, afterglow at most 2000, so `T_F ≤ 5300` ms (about 1.3 s at the low end); for a boss blow the stillness is up to 350 and the echo hold extends the afterglow to up to 3000, so `T_F ≤ 6450` ms. *Example:* `Q = 40` gives a build of `⌈40 / 3⌉ × 160 = 2240` ms and `T_F ≤ 5940`. With `k` Detonations a Skip lasts at most `T_skip(k) = k × (202 + 300 + T_F) + 440` ms, the 300 being the orient hold (plus the ready phases). *Examples* (`Q ≤ 29`, no boss blow): `k = 0`: 440 ms; `k = 1`: 6,242 ms; `k = 5`: 29,450 ms. A Skip across many Detonations is slower than it feels, so System 35 reports the 95th percentile of forced seconds per fight as a trip-wire and owns its threshold; if Detonations are common the Detonation thresholds are retuned, never Skip.

**Formula 9: the boss blow.** For a root action `r` in the boss fight: `D_boss(r)` = the boss unit's actual shield lost plus HP lost in `r`; `start(r)` = the boss's HP plus shield at `ViewAt(first(r) - 1)`; `bossMax` = the boss's maximum HP from the baseline view; `X` = the registry value `run_defining_damage_share`. `r` qualifies iff `level(r) = Detonation` and `100 × D_boss(r) ≥ X × bossMax` and `100 × start(r) ≥ X × bossMax`; both comparisons are integer and inclusive. The share shown is `⌊100 × D_boss / bossMax⌋`. *Examples:* `bossMax = 40`, `X = 50`: `D_boss = 20` qualifies (`2000 ≥ 2000`) and `19` does not; 12 shield plus 8 HP counts as 20; `start = 18` with `D_boss = 20` does not qualify (`1800 < 2000`). Overkill is excluded (only actual loss counts); Formula 1's `D` and `Hmax` cover all living enemies and are not reused.

| Variable | Type | Range | Source | Description |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `D_boss` | int | 0 to the boss's HP plus shield | System 36 views | Actual damage to the boss in one root action. |
| `start` | int | 0 to the boss's HP plus shield | System 36 view | The boss's HP plus shield when the action began. |
| `bossMax` | int | 1 and over | Baseline view | The boss's maximum HP. |
| `X` | int percent | 35 (the classifier's floor, Rule 4) to 70 (the registry's safe range) | Registry | A definition constant; changes need the creative director's sign-off. |

## Edge Cases

Format: *condition: outcome.*

**Trace and states**
- *A zero-record encounter (only `CombatEnded`):* one terminal beat; `PlaybackFinished` is sent once.
- *`Begin` on an unsealed trace:* rejected; nothing plays.
- *A technical stop at sequence 47:* exactly 47 records are presented, the state is `Stopped`, no outcome and no Defeat is shown, Skip stops at the Detonations inside the prefix, then lands at record 47; a stop with an empty prefix goes `Idle → Stopped`.
- *A `ChainLimit` fact in a chain:* a normal short beat; the root action is classified without it counting as a mutating effect.
- *A Draw (both sides downed):* the same terminal path; the banner reads Draw; no extra effect.
- *The 200-action cap:* playback ends at the terminal record like any other fight; level-aware pacing keeps it short, and Skip reduces it to the cuts and the Forced spans of its Detonations (Formula 8).
- *Duplicate hand-off:* `PlaybackFinished` is idempotent per encounter; a second call is a no-op.
- *Abandon while Playing, Skipping or in a Forced span:* `SkipArmed` is cleared, the cursor is discarded, no outcome is delivered, the director goes `Idle` (System 5 owns the abandon).

**Pacing and speed**
- *Speed changed mid-beat:* takes effect at the next beat boundary; the current beat keeps its progress fraction.
- *Skip on the last beat:* the final leg is empty; the sweep and the banner still play, and if the last root action is a Detonation (a lethal blow) the banner waits for the end of its afterglow, the same in full playback and in Skip.
- *Skip pressed while armed (a fast-forward leg or a Forced span):* ignored, with a one-frame pressed acknowledgement and no luminance rise; key repeat never counts. Keys 1, 2, 3 or a speed segment cancel the arm.
- *A hitch (a very long frame):* the delta is clamped; several short beats may be crossed in one frame; none is double-fired.
- *Window deactivation:* `InputReset`; the clock pauses and the menu opens; nothing catches up on return (no burst).
- *The menu opened during the stillness beat or the bloom:* the clock stops mid-effect; resume fades in over 250 ms.
- *Two Detonations within 500 ms (two heroes, back to back):* the second degrades to Surge (Formula 6). In Skip this cannot happen (consecutive blooms are at least about 1.3 s apart, Rule 10); the rule stays as a defensive guard.
- *A very large chain at 4×:* beats group in threes (and merge past 29 in the Vertical Slice; in the MVP they play unmerged); chips aggregate per target.
- *A fight with no Detonation:* Skip is one fast-forward leg (the settle sweep) and the banner; a Surge never stops it.
- *The first root action is a Detonation:* the leading leg is empty and dropped, and the span plays as in full playback with the 4× build.
- *Back-to-back Detonations:* the leg between them is empty, with no cut, hold or reset; the second Forced span starts right after the first afterglow.
- *Skip at 249 ms:* refused, not buffered. *At 251 ms inside a Detonation root action:* the rest of that root action is a Forced span; inside any other root action the rest of the beat is dropped and the cursor jumps.
- *A Detonation the flash budget would degrade to Surge:* still a stop; it plays the stillness beat and the Surge form.
- *A technical-stop prefix that ends inside a root action:* that root action is classified from the records it has and may not be a Detonation, as in full playback of the prefix.
- *The menu opened during a fast-forward leg or a Forced span:* the clock stops, `SkipArmed` survives the pause, and the leg or span continues after the 250 ms fade-in.
- *Speed changed while armed:* keys 1, 2, 3 or a speed segment cancel the arm (a Forced span in progress plays to its end first); no other input changes it.
- *"Skip goes straight to the end" is on:* Skip is one leg to the terminal view with the settle sweep and the banner; it never stops at a Detonation and has no Forced span.
- *Records of one root action are not contiguous:* `BuildSkipPlan` asserts contiguity; if it fails the span is `[min first, max last]` of the root and a defect is reported to System 4 (the Event Log states only that sequence numbers are contiguous).

**Presentation**
- *A target downed mid-chain:* the unit fades without a luminance rise and the enemy lines do not reflow; later effects aimed at it follow the recorded `EffectSkipped`.
- *Several targets in one detonation:* bursts are staggered 60 to 80 ms in line order; only one bright core rises at a time.
- *A stunned enemy:* its intent glyph slot stays dark for the beat (the frame is a new art item, listed below).
- *Details open when the workbench follows another hero:* retained if the source stays valid and the reading area is safe (Combat HUD Rule 6); closed at every Skip fast-forward leg.
- *A 24 px four-digit aggregate numeral overlapping a neighbour body:* allowed; it never overlaps a tab or an HP row, and it sits below the protected layer.
- *The stillness beat in a classified Detonation that the flash budget refuses:* the stillness beat still plays; the bloom is replaced by the Surge form.
- *A boss blow followed by a Defeat:* the beat has already played; System 30 does not call it run-defining. *No boss in the roster:* nothing is marked. *A boss with shield:* shield lost counts in `D_boss`.

**Settings and accessibility**
- *Reduced flashes on:* steady glow instead of the bloom, no particles or embers, rings in 3 steps; the schedule and the information are unchanged.
- *Reduced motion on:* fades of equal duration; the timing is unchanged.
- *Muted audio:* the outcome and the actor are still identifiable from text and form.
- *The language changes (only possible via the menu):* labels re-resolve in place; playback does not restart; the cursor and the paused flag are kept.
- *The first fight with Reduced flashes still on its default:* the prompt has been shown once before the fight; the default is on until the player turns it off.

## Game Feel

The payoff is the game's loudest moment, and it must read as earned.

- **Quiet by default.** A Tick is a murmur. The player should feel the difference between a click and a bloom without reading anything.
- **Rise, hold, breath.** A Chain rises link by link; a Detonation takes a breath (the stillness beat), builds, and releases. The release is the only time the scene reaches the light ceiling.
- **The cause stays lit.** After the bloom the plates that mattered glow again in order, so the satisfaction is also an explanation.
- **Respect for time.** Boring actions are brief, big ones are not; Skip is always available and always honest, and it never passes a Detonation; pausing is free.
- **No latency from the presenter.** It reads and schedules; a beat is drawn from pooled objects with no allocation in steady state.

## Acceptance Criteria

Evidence: **Logic** (the director, the scheduler, the classifier) as automated EditMode tests in `Assets/Tests/EditMode/Playback/` (class `PlaybackTests`), named `[Scenario]_[Expected]`, gate BLOCKING; **Visual/Feel** and **UI** as retained screenshots or capture clips in `production/qa/evidence/` with lead sign-off (BLOCKING); the binder's lifetime as Play Mode evidence. Fixtures: `GoldenTraces` (the event-log Seal example, a 200-action trace, a technical-stop prefix at sequence 47, a zero-record trace), `FakeClock` (an integer tick), `FakeGate` (counts `PlaybackFinished`), `FakeView` (`ViewAt` stub), `BeatHash` (a stable hash of a schedule), `AllocProbe`. Specific values are provisional with the thresholds.

| # | Given / When / Then |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Determinism.** The same trace and the same speed sequence give an identical `Beat[]` hash on two runs; the schedule uses no clock or RNG. |
| 2 | **Skip parity.** After any Skip plan runs to the end the final view equals the view after full playback (record for record), and each Forced span equals the same root action in full playback; the trace and the outcome are unchanged by any speed sequence. |
| 3 | **Classification.** Golden roots give: a single hit Tick; three chained effects with depth 2 Chain; `N = 7` with no detonation Surge; a Seal detonation with `N = 3`, `D = 12`, `Hmax = 40` Chain; the same with `D = 14` Detonation; a detonation that is lethal to the last enemy Detonation; a chain with `N = 12` and no detonation Detonation by the size route and `N = 11` Surge; `EffectSkipped`, `ListenerSuppressed` and `DetonationEmpty` do not count in `N`. |
| 4 | **One root only.** Classification of a root action reads no record outside its `rootActionId` and never `CombatEnded` (a spy on the trace reader). |
| 5 | **Cadence.** `cadence(260, 8) = 260`, `cadence(320, 40) = 120`, `cadence(400, 1000) = 120`; 2× gives 130 for the first; 8 triggers at 4× take 480 ms. |
| 6 | **[VS] Queue cap.** 40 effects at base 320 give 29 beats and one merged aggregate; in the MVP all 40 play unmerged at the 120 ms floor. |
| 7 | **Level-aware pacing.** A skipped action lasts at most 300 ms; a Tick-only Swing of 240 ms lasts 120 ms at 1×, and at 2× and 4× Tick-only beats form groups of 3 held 160 ms (the effective speed is capped at 4×); a Chain keeps the chosen speed; a Detonation keeps its authored timing at 1×, 2× and 4×. |
| 8 | **Rise floor.** No luminance rise in any schedule is under 100 ms at 1×, 2×, 4× or Skip. |
| 9 | **Speed change.** A speed change mid-beat applies from the next beat boundary and keeps the current beat's progress fraction. |
| 10 | **Flash budget.** Blooms at 0 and 400 ms: the second degrades to Surge; at 0 and 600 ms both play; no rolling 1000 ms window holds more than 2 flash-class events at any speed. |
| 11 | **Skip plan.** `BuildSkipPlan` is pure and returns, for a 200-action trace with Detonations at actions 3 and 9 and cursor 0, the segments FF, Forced, FF, Forced, FF with strictly increasing targets (`firstSeq - 1` before each Forced span, `lastSeq` at its end); zero Detonations give one FF; a Detonation at action 1 has no leading FF; a Detonation at the last action leaves a final FF holding only `CombatEnded`; five adjacent Detonations give 5 Forced spans and no inner FF; a Surge never stops; a Detonation the flash budget degrades still stops and plays its Surge form. |
| 12 | **Pause.** Opening the menu stops the cursor; closing it resumes after a 250 ms fade-in; a deactivation pauses and opens the menu and never produces a catch-up burst; a language change while paused re-resolves without restarting. |
| 13 | **Terminal once.** A natural end and a final Skip leg each send `PlaybackFinished` exactly once, and `ViewChanged(t)` once; a Skip stop or handover sends neither; a second call is a no-op. |
| 14 | **Technical stop.** A stop at sequence 47 presents exactly 47 records, ends `Stopped`, shows no outcome, and Skip stops at the Detonation roots inside the prefix and then lands at record 47; an empty prefix goes `Idle → Stopped`. |
| 15 | **Unsealed and abandon.** `Begin` on an unsealed trace is rejected; an abandon in `Playing`, `Skipping` or a Forced span clears `SkipArmed`, discards the cursor and delivers no outcome. |
| 16 | **Hitch.** A frame delta over `MaxFrameDeltaMs` is clamped; no beat is fired twice (`lastFiredBeatId`); the cursor never moves backwards or beyond the sealed end under any sequence of Advance, speed and Skip calls. |
| 17 | **Frozen arrangement.** During playback System 25's source is `FrozenArrangement` even when the live grid is Editable; the record is not released by System 29. |
| 18 | **Audio rules.** Gains match Formula 7 (a Chain cue at 2× is 284 permille; pitch stepping is [VS]); a Tick never steals a voice; the voice limits hold; the retrigger interval is `max(60, cadence / speed)`; in a Skip fast-forward leg no per-event cue plays and a Forced span plays all of them; the Detonation level is the loudest in a bus-level test. |
| 19 | **No sound-only information.** Every cue has a visual or text counterpart in the view model (level, numeral, outcome, actor). |
| 20 | **Reduced flashes and motion.** With Reduced flashes the schedule has no bloom, particles or embers, the Forced-span bloom and the boss blow bloom are steady glows, and pop-ins aggregate at 2× and 4×; with Reduced motion every travel becomes a fade of equal duration; the schedule's timing and the information are identical to the default. |
| 21 | **Presenter purity.** The playback assemblies have `noEngineReferences`, no mutable statics, no wall clock, and no write path to Systems 3, 4 or 36 (an assembly reference check). |
| 22 | **Allocation (ADVISORY).** A 200-action playback allocates 0 bytes in steady state (an `AllocProbe` with an allocating control). |
| 23 | **Controls.** Keys 1, 2, 3 map to speed and cancel an armed Skip, and K arms Skip; Space on a focused segment never skips; only the speed segments, the intent tabs and the menu are focusable during playback. |
| 24 | **Ladder visuals (Visual evidence).** Retained captures of each level on the real grid: Tick, Chain, Surge, Detonation with the stillness beat, the fan, the bloom and the afterglow with the contributing plates lit; the protected layer is never covered; a greyscale pass. [BLOCKED: a build and an analyser] |
| 26 | **Long fight.** The median fight length over the harness set is at most 90 s at 1× (ADVISORY until the harness exists). [BLOCKED: the headless harness and real action counts] |
| 27 | **Play Mode.** Subscriptions pair across two enter and exit cycles with the domain reload off; effects spawn once per beat; no allocation per tick. [BLOCKED: the UI toolkit decision] |
| 28 | **Skip behaviour.** Skip is refused at 249 ms (not buffered) and accepted at 251 ms. A fast-forward leg closes Details, emits no per-event effect or cue, releases pools and echoes, bumps `GenerationId` once per jump and once per handover, and shows the sweep and "Skipped N actions" for the orient hold. A Forced span re-enables the workbench follow, plays every cue in full and ignores a Skip press and key repeat. Keys 1, 2, 3 cancel the arm from the next beat boundary and never cut a Forced span. Skip accepted inside a Detonation root action forces the rest of it; inside another root action it jumps at once. |
| 29 | **Skip timing.** `T_cut(36, 20) = 406` ms, `T_cut(36, 6) = 202` ms, `T_cut(0, F) = 0`; `T_skip(0) = 440` ms, and for `Q ≤ 29` without a boss blow `T_skip(1) ≤ 6,242` ms and `T_skip(5) ≤ 29,450` ms; `T_F ≤ 5300` ms (6,450 for a boss blow) without the ready phase, and `Q = 40` gives a build of 2,240 ms. |
| 30 | **Skip flash safety.** Consecutive Forced blooms are at least 1,650 ms apart with a cut and a hold between them and at least 1,310 ms apart back to back, no rolling 1000 ms window holds more than one, and the sweep is not flash-class (Visual evidence: the analyser clips of Visual/Audio with zero failures). [BLOCKED: a build and an analyser] |
| 31 | **Skip edge states.** A technical stop at 47 with a Detonation root inside forces it, ends `Stopped` at 47 and shows no banner; a root cut by the stop is classified from the records it has; the menu opened during a leg or a Forced span keeps `SkipArmed` and resumes after the fade-in; with "Skip goes straight to the end" on, Skip is one leg and never stops at a Detonation, and with it off Skip stops at every Detonation. |
| 32 | **Boss blow.** With `bossMax = 40` and `X = 50`: `D_boss = 20` with `start = 40` is marked and `19` is not; 12 shield plus 8 HP counts as 20; `start = 18` with `D_boss = 20` is not marked; a surviving boss, a Detonation in an earlier room, or a non-boss fight marks nothing; every qualifying root is marked in order, with no look at later roots; changing `X` to 60 changes the verdict with no code change; a boss blow is marked even when the party then loses; System 29 and the harness agree on a golden set; classification reads no record outside the root action and never `CombatEnded`. |
| 33 | **Boss blow and Skip visuals (Visual evidence).** Retained clips of a Skip with two Detonations and of the boss blow (with Reduced flashes and Reduced motion variants) show the full stillness, bloom and afterglow, the share line, the HP outline and the held echoes identical to full playback, with the banner after the afterglow; a thumbnail test tells the boss blow's stillness frame from an ordinary Detonation's without reading text, and the concept's playtest proxy (testers watch a scripted run-defining run and say whether it reads as different) uses the same clip. [BLOCKED: a build, an analyser, the art bible amendments and the art director's mock-up] |
